# Formal Study Outline

## 1. Study Positioning

This study is a qualitative-dominant mixed-methods field deployment of MetaPlan, a GenAI-supported task management tool designed to scaffold metacognitive processes for university students with ADHD.

The study does not aim to establish causal efficacy through a controlled experiment. Instead, it examines how participants' metacognitive processes unfold in real-world task management contexts as they interact with GenAI scaffolding over a two-week deployment.

The central focus is on process, interaction, appropriation, and boundary conditions:

- how participants plan, initiate, monitor, recover from breakdowns, and reflect on tasks;
- how GenAI scaffolding enters, supports, interrupts, or reshapes these processes;
- how participants adopt, modify, ignore, resist, or abandon AI-generated suggestions;
- how MetaPlan fits into participants' existing task management ecology;
- what design principles and boundaries emerge for GenAI metacognitive scaffolding for ADHD users.

## 2. Research Question

**RQ: How does the metacognitive process of university students with ADHD unfold as they interact with GenAI scaffolding for task management?**

This RQ foregrounds process rather than outcome. It asks how planning, execution, monitoring, regulation, and reflection take shape through interaction with GenAI scaffolding in everyday academic and work-related tasks.

Possible analytic sub-questions:

1. How do participants use MetaPlan to externalize, structure, and prioritize tasks?
2. How do participants respond to GenAI scaffolding when initiating tasks, getting stuck, or reflecting on task progress?
3. When do participants adopt, adapt, ignore, or resist AI-generated suggestions?
4. How does MetaPlan interact with participants' pre-existing task management tools and habits?
5. What tensions, burdens, and boundaries appear when GenAI scaffolding is used for ADHD-related task management difficulties?

## 3. Participants

The study will recruit **25 university students with diagnosed ADHD**, including undergraduate, master's, and doctoral students.

### 3.1 Inclusion Criteria

Participants must meet all of the following criteria:

- currently enrolled as an undergraduate, master's, or doctoral student;
- formally diagnosed with ADHD;
- has an existing habit of task management, such as using paper-based or digital tools;
- has used some form of task management at least once per week during the past three months;
- primarily completes daily study or work activities on a computer;
- uses a computer for study or work-related activities for an average of at least 4 hours per day;
- uses a Windows computer compatible with MetaPlan.

### 3.2 Exclusion Criteria

Participants will be excluded if they:

- cannot use their personal computer consistently during the two-week deployment;
- are unwilling to have system behavior logs and AI interaction traces collected;
- are currently experiencing a severe mental health crisis, as identified through self-report or researcher judgment during screening;
- cannot complete the onboarding, deployment, or post-study interview in the study language.

### 3.3 Recruitment Channels

Potential recruitment channels include:

- university disability support offices or counseling center referrals;
- ADHD-related student communities;
- social media posts in ADHD-related groups;
- snowball sampling through eligible participants.

### 3.4 Sampling Rationale

The target sample size of 25 participants is intended to support cross-case qualitative analysis while allowing variation across degree stage, task management habits, ADHD-related challenges, and engagement patterns during deployment.

The study should not treat low engagement as simple failure. Participants who use MetaPlan lightly, stop using it, or return to prior tools may reveal important boundary conditions for GenAI scaffolding in ADHD task management.

## 5. Pre-study Questionnaire

The pre-study questionnaire will be completed before Day 0. It should take approximately 5-8 minutes.

The purpose is not to measure intervention outcomes. Instead, it collects contextual baseline information about participants' existing task management ecology, ADHD background, computer-based work routines, and willingness to discuss task management artifacts during the pre-study session.

### 5.1 Background Information

The questionnaire should collect:

- age;
- gender, if participants are willing to disclose;
- degree stage: undergraduate, master's, or doctoral;
- discipline or major;
- ADHD diagnosis status and diagnosis timing, if participants are willing to disclose;
- medication status, if participants are willing to disclose;
- primary ADHD-related task management difficulties, such as task initiation, sustained attention, task switching, time perception, procrastination, working memory, or reflection.

### 5.2 Current Task Management Ecology

The questionnaire should collect descriptive information about participants' existing task management practices:

- what tools they currently use, such as paper planners, notebooks, sticky notes, calendars, Notion, Todo apps, reminders, documents, spreadsheets, chat messages, screenshots, or mental tracking;
- which tool, if any, is their primary task management tool;
- whether they use multiple tools together;
- how often they have used task management tools or methods during the past three months;
- whether they have previously used AI tools such as ChatGPT, Notion AI, Copilot, or other assistants for task planning, task decomposition, reminders, or reflection.

This section should avoid creating unvalidated baseline scales. Its function is to characterize participants' task management ecology before MetaPlan.

### 5.3 Artifact Availability and Consent

The questionnaire should ask whether participants would be comfortable discussing or optionally showing a current task management artifact during the Day 0 session.

Example wording:

> If you are willing, we may ask you during the interview to describe or show a current task management artifact, such as a to-do list, calendar, note, screenshot, planner, or paper record. You can refuse, describe without showing, or hide any private information before showing it.

Suggested response options:

- willing to show an actual artifact;
- willing to describe, but not show, an artifact;
- unsure, will decide during the interview;
- not willing to discuss or show an artifact.

Participants should not be required to upload screenshots or artifacts in advance.

## 6. Day 0: Pre-study Session

The Day 0 session should take approximately 60-90 minutes.

### 6.1 Informed Consent

The researcher will explain:

- study purpose and procedure;
- expected duration and participant activities;
- what data will be collected;
- system behavior logging;
- AI interaction trace collection;
- interview recording and transcription;
- data storage, anonymization, and deletion;
- participants' right to withdraw at any time;
- that the study evaluates the tool and design, not participants' abilities or performance.

Because the tool includes AI interaction, the consent process should also explain:

- participants should avoid entering highly sensitive personal information when possible;
- AI conversation traces may be reviewed by researchers after anonymization;
- any third-party AI or cloud service involved in data processing should be disclosed;
- participants may request deletion of their data.

### 6.2 MAI-19 Pre-test

Participants will complete the MAI-19 task-management-adapted measure before using MetaPlan.

The MAI-19 should be framed as an exploratory descriptive measure. It is used to contextualize participants' metacognitive awareness related to task management and to observe possible pre-post change signals. It is not the primary evidence for tool effectiveness.

### 6.3 Baseline Artifact or Ecology Walkthrough

Before introducing MetaPlan, the researcher will conduct a baseline walkthrough of the participant's current task management practice.

The format depends on the participant's preference:

- if the participant is willing to show an artifact, the researcher asks them to walk through a current to-do list, calendar, planner, note, digital tool, or other task management material;
- if the participant is willing to describe but not show, the researcher asks them to describe the structure and use of their current system;
- if the participant does not use external artifacts, the absence of a fixed external task management system is documented as part of the baseline.

The walkthrough should explore:

- how tasks are captured;
- how tasks are prioritized;
- whether tasks are decomposed into actionable steps;
- how deadlines and urgency are represented;
- how unfinished tasks are handled;
- how participants recover from being stuck or distracted;
- whether there is any review or reflection practice;
- what parts of the current system help;
- what parts often break down.

This baseline is interpretive rather than evaluative. It anchors later analysis of how MetaPlan is appropriated, resisted, or compared with prior practices.

### 6.4 Recent Episode Elicitation

The researcher will ask participants to describe two recent task management episodes:

1. one episode where task management went relatively well;
2. one episode where they were stuck, delayed, overwhelmed, or unable to proceed as intended.

For each episode, the researcher should ask:

- what the task was;
- how the participant knew they needed to do it;
- what the first concrete action was;
- what tools, supports, or strategies were used;
- where the process went smoothly or broke down;
- how the episode ended;
- what the participant took away from it.

These episodes provide concrete baseline materials for comparison during the post-study interview.

### 6.5 Deployment-start Task Inventory

Before onboarding, participants will identify 3-5 real tasks currently present in their academic or work life.

For each task, the researcher records:

- task name or description;
- deadline or time sensitivity;
- current status;
- whether it has already been broken down into steps;
- why it feels important, difficult, or easy to avoid;
- whether the participant might want to bring it into MetaPlan.

The task inventory allows the Day 15 interview to revisit how these real tasks evolved, stalled, disappeared, or were restructured during deployment.

### 6.6 MetaPlan Installation and Onboarding

The researcher will help participants install MetaPlan and complete basic onboarding.

Onboarding should cover:

- creating and editing tasks;
- using task hierarchy or decomposition if supported;
- starting a first step;
- accessing stuck support;
- accessing reflection;
- understanding what data is logged;
- confirming that data synchronization works.

The researcher should explain feature locations and basic operations, but avoid prescribing an ideal way to use MetaPlan. The goal is to preserve naturalistic appropriation during deployment.

## 7. Day 1-14: Naturalistic Field Deployment

Participants will use MetaPlan in their real study and work contexts for two weeks.

### 7.1 Deployment Principle

Participants are encouraged to use MetaPlan naturally when it feels relevant to their own task management. The study should avoid presenting usage as a performance requirement.

Recommended wording:

> Please use MetaPlan as naturally as possible in your daily study or work tasks. You do not need to use it perfectly or every day. Forgetting, stopping, switching back to other tools, or finding it unhelpful are also useful for the study, because we are interested in what actually happens in real life.

### 7.2 Engagement Tracking

The study may describe recommended engagement, but should avoid rigid usage requirements that conflict with naturalistic use.

For analysis, cases can be described by engagement level:

- high engagement;
- moderate engagement;
- low engagement;
- discontinuation or non-use.

Low engagement should be analyzed as a possible boundary condition rather than automatically discarded.

### 7.3 Researcher Role

During deployment, the researcher will:

- provide technical support when needed;
- respond to participant questions;
- avoid instructing participants to use specific features;
- avoid nudging participants toward "better" or more frequent use;
- check in only when there are technical issues, prolonged non-use, or scheduled check-in points.

If a participant does not use MetaPlan for several days, the researcher may contact them with neutral wording, such as:

> I noticed there may have been little activity recently. I just wanted to check whether there was any technical issue or anything uncomfortable about using the tool. It is also okay if you simply did not find it useful.

## 8. System Logging During Deployment

The system logging plan should align directly with the research question.

### 8.1 General Behavior Logs

MetaPlan should record:

- application open and close events;
- active usage duration;
- task creation, editing, deletion, completion;
- task hierarchy changes;
- priority changes;
- task order changes;
- focus session start, pause, resume, and end;
- reflection entry start and completion.

### 8.2 GenAI Scaffolding Interaction Logs

For each GenAI scaffolding event, MetaPlan should record:

- trigger context, such as planning, first-step initiation, stuck support, or reflection;
- user input or selected task context;
- AI-generated output;
- whether the participant selected, accepted, copied, modified, ignored, regenerated, or dismissed the suggestion;
- any explicit feedback, if available;
- immediate follow-up behavior, such as continuing a task, editing the task, completing a step, asking for more support, or leaving the tool.

### 8.3 Analytic Purpose of Logs

Logs should not only be used to count feature frequency. They should support process-oriented analysis of:

- when participants seek scaffolding;
- how AI suggestions are taken up or resisted;
- whether scaffolding leads to task continuation, task restructuring, or abandonment;
- how planning, execution, and reflection unfold over time.

## 9. Day 7-8: Mid-study Check-in

Instead of daily micro-surveys, the study will collect a mid-study check-in questionnaire around Day 7 or Day 8.

The purpose is to capture critical incidents, early adoption patterns, non-use, technical issues, and perceived burden without imposing daily reporting demands.

The check-in should take approximately 5 minutes.

Suggested questions:

1. In the past week, when did you most often open or use MetaPlan?
2. Please briefly describe one moment when MetaPlan helped you start, continue, organize, or reflect on a task.
3. Please briefly describe one moment when MetaPlan did not help, felt annoying, increased pressure, or was not worth using.
4. Were there any functions you rarely used or avoided? Why?
5. Did you encounter any technical problems, privacy concerns, or moments of discomfort?
6. Is there anything you would like the researcher to know before the second week?

The researcher may also send a brief non-directive message to confirm that the participant is not experiencing technical or emotional burden.

## 10. Day 9-14: Continued Deployment

Participants continue naturalistic use during the second week.

The second week is especially useful for observing:

- whether initial novelty fades;
- whether participants develop stable use routines;
- whether they return to previous tools;
- whether AI scaffolding is integrated, ignored, or resisted;
- whether reflection becomes meaningful or burdensome;
- whether the tool creates additional task management overhead.

## 11. End-study Check-in Questionnaire

At the end of Day 14 or before the Day 15 interview, participants complete an end-study check-in questionnaire.

The purpose is to help participants recall critical episodes and prepare materials for the post-study trace elicitation interview.

Suggested questions:

1. What types of tasks did you most often use MetaPlan for during the two weeks?
2. Please describe the most helpful moment you remember.
3. Please describe the least helpful, most frustrating, or most burdensome moment you remember.
4. Did MetaPlan change how you recorded, decomposed, started, monitored, or reflected on tasks?
5. Did you stop using or avoid any feature? Why?
6. Were there situations where you did not want AI to intervene?
7. Is there anything you would want to keep using after the study?
8. Is there anything you would not want in a long-term version of this tool?

## 12. Day 15: Post-study Session

The Day 15 session should take approximately 60-90 minutes.

### 12.1 MAI-19 Post-test

Participants complete the same MAI-19 measure as in the pre-test.

The pre-post comparison is treated as exploratory and descriptive. It should not be used as the central claim of effectiveness.

### 12.2 Trace Elicitation Interview

Before the interview, the researcher prepares 3-5 trace episodes using:

- system behavior logs;
- AI interaction traces;
- Mid-study check-in responses;
- End-study check-in responses;
- Day 0 task inventory;
- notable periods of high engagement, low engagement, discontinuation, or return to prior tools.

Potential trace episodes include:

- first use of an AI first-step suggestion;
- repeated use of stuck support;
- a reflection session with rich content;
- an ignored or dismissed AI suggestion;
- a task that was repeatedly delayed;
- a sudden increase or decrease in usage;
- a Day 0 inventory task that was completed, reframed, postponed, or abandoned.

For each episode, the researcher asks:

- do you remember what was happening here?
- what were you trying to do?
- what did the AI suggestion mean to you at that moment?
- did you accept, modify, ignore, or resist it?
- did it help you continue, or did it interrupt you?
- what might you have done without MetaPlan?

### 12.3 Comparison With Baseline

The researcher revisits the Day 0 baseline materials:

- current task management ecology;
- artifact walkthrough;
- recent successful and difficult episodes;
- deployment-start task inventory.

Possible questions:

- Compared with the way you managed tasks before MetaPlan, what felt different during these two weeks?
- Did any of your prior tools remain more useful than MetaPlan?
- Did MetaPlan replace, supplement, or compete with your existing tools?
- What happened to the tasks you listed at the beginning of the study?
- Did any task get decomposed, restarted, reframed, completed, delayed, or abandoned through MetaPlan?

### 12.4 Feature-level Discussion

The researcher discusses major MetaPlan components:

- task list and task organization;
- first-step scaffolding;
- stuck support;
- focus or execution support;
- reflection;
- AI-generated suggestions.

For each component, ask:

- what did this component help with?
- when was it most useful?
- when was it not useful?
- did it add burden or pressure?
- did it feel supportive, controlling, irrelevant, or interruptive?
- how would you change it?

### 12.5 Boundary and Long-term Use Discussion

This section directly addresses design boundaries.

Suggested questions:

- When should AI not intervene in task management?
- Were there moments when AI suggestions felt too generic, too demanding, judgmental, or misaligned?
- Did the tool ever make task management feel like another task?
- Did it ever increase pressure, guilt, or avoidance?
- Did it ever reduce your sense of agency?
- What ADHD-related difficulties did the tool not address?
- What would worry you about using this kind of tool long term?
- What should designers be careful about when building GenAI scaffolding for ADHD students?

## 13. Data Sources

The study will collect:

- pre-study questionnaire;
- MAI-19 pre-test and post-test;
- Day 0 baseline artifact or ecology walkthrough;
- recent episode elicitation;
- deployment-start task inventory;
- system behavior logs;
- GenAI scaffolding interaction traces;
- Mid-study check-in questionnaire;
- End-study check-in questionnaire;
- Day 15 trace elicitation interview;
- researcher field notes;
- optional expert feedback.

## 14. Analysis Plan

### 14.1 Qualitative Analysis

The primary analysis will use Reflexive Thematic Analysis.

The analysis will focus on:

- how participants' metacognitive processes unfold through interaction with MetaPlan;
- how planning, execution, monitoring, regulation, and reflection appear in real use;
- how participants appropriate or resist AI scaffolding;
- how AI suggestions become useful, irrelevant, burdensome, or disruptive;
- how MetaPlan interacts with existing task management practices;
- how low engagement and discontinuation reveal design boundaries.

### 14.2 Trace-based Case Analysis

For each participant, the researcher will build a case summary combining:

- baseline task management ecology;
- deployment-start task inventory;
- engagement pattern;
- key AI interaction episodes;
- mid-study and end-study check-in responses;
- post-study interview themes.

Cross-case comparison can then examine differences across:

- degree stage;
- existing task management systems;
- types of ADHD-related difficulties;
- high, moderate, and low engagement cases;
- participants who accepted AI support versus those who modified, ignored, or resisted it.

### 14.3 Quantitative and Descriptive Analysis

Quantitative analysis is secondary and descriptive.

Possible descriptive analyses:

- MAI-19 pre-post change as exploratory signal;
- application usage days;
- active usage duration;
- task creation and completion counts;
- frequency of first-step scaffolding, stuck support, and reflection;
- AI suggestion acceptance, modification, dismissal, and regeneration patterns;
- engagement trajectories across the two weeks.

These analyses should support interpretation of qualitative findings rather than serve as standalone effectiveness claims.

## 15. Ethics and Participant Care

The study involves ADHD participants and AI-mediated task management, so care should be taken to reduce burden, pressure, and privacy risk.

Key principles:

- participation should not be framed as performance evaluation;
- low use or non-use should be treated as valid study data;
- participants can refuse to show artifacts;
- participants can redact private information before showing any materials;
- participants should not be required to complete daily surveys;
- researcher messages should avoid guilt-inducing language;
- participants can withdraw at any time;
- mental health support resources should be provided if distress appears;
- AI interaction data should be anonymized before analysis;
- sensitive personal information should be minimized where possible.

## 16. Reviewer-facing Rationale

This design should be framed as a field deployment rather than a controlled effectiveness study.

Suggested rationale:

> Because the study aims to understand how metacognitive processes unfold through real-world interaction with GenAI scaffolding, it does not rely on a control condition or intensive baseline tracking. Instead, it collects a contextual baseline through participants' existing task management ecology, optional artifact walkthroughs, recent task management episodes, and a deployment-start task inventory. These materials serve as interpretive anchors for analyzing how participants appropriate, adapt, resist, or abandon MetaPlan during a two-week deployment.

The main contribution should be grounded in process-level qualitative findings, trace-based interaction analysis, and design implications for GenAI metacognitive scaffolding for ADHD university students.
